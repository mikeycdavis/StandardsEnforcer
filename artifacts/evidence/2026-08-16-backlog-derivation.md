# The backlog tracker's derivation, made executable

**Date:** 2026-08-16
**Item:** [ST-10](../backlog/items/ST-10.md) — *The backlog tracker claims to be derived, and no
executable derivation exists*
**Parent:** [EP-06](../backlog/items/EP-06.md) — self-assurance
**Branch:** `backlog/st-10-executable-derivation`, from `main` at `c974cb3`

## What was wrong

`artifacts/backlog/README.md` opened with `GENERATED FILE - do not edit by hand. Re-run the backlog
script after changing anything in items/.` There was no backlog script. `package.json` exposed only
`npm test`, and nothing in the repository produced the page. The derivation was a human procedure,
performed by whoever last edited an item, and checked by nobody.

That makes a stale figure and a computed one indistinguishable on the surface [IN-01](../backlog/items/IN-01.md)
uses to decide what work exists. It is EP-06's property — an assurance surface must establish what it
reports — applied to the record of the work rather than to the product.

## Why it was worth doing before the other two open items

ST-10 was filed alongside [ST-08](../backlog/items/ST-08.md) (symlink capability) and
[ST-09](../backlog/items/ST-09.md) (`ci.sh` under Git Bash), and is not the most severe of the three.
It went first because the evidence for it strengthened after it was filed. Between `c45a4e4` and
`c974cb3` the tracker's figures were hand-recomputed and hand-edited on three separate occasions, and
the post-merge verification for two pull requests had to independently re-derive the same numbers
from `items/` in order to believe them. The repository was actively depending on a manual derivation
that nothing enforced — which is a different claim from "this could drift one day".

The surface it protects also has leverage over every later prioritisation decision, including the
ordering of ST-08 and ST-09 themselves.

## What was built

A **checker**, not a generator. `scripts/backlog.mjs` recomputes every mechanical claim on the
tracker from `artifacts/backlog/items/*.md` and reports each disagreement, naming the specific
figure. `test/backlog-tracker.test.mjs` runs it as part of `npm test`.

```text
items/*.md frontmatter  ──derive──▶  expected figures
                                          │
   artifacts/backlog/README.md ────── compare ──▶ findings, one per wrong figure
```

The tracker carries editorial prose the item files do not contain — the "Answers" column, the
section wording, the introduction. A generator owns the whole page and turns that prose into
generator source; a checker recomputes only what is genuinely derived and leaves the rest alone. The
precedent is FinancialStandards' `scripts/inventory.mjs`, which compares rather than derives so that
two copies cannot silently disagree. If too much of the page later proves mechanical, *that* is the
evidence for generation. It was not assumed at the start.

**Hierarchy semantics come from frontmatter alone.** Parenthood, depth, leafness and completion are
read from `parent:` and `status:`. The README never tells the checker what the shape of the backlog
is; it is the rendered assertion being checked.

**What is derived** — leaf ratio and percentage, the progress bar, the four status counts and the
total, the six hierarchy type counts, the per-theme progress row, the in-flight list, the
ready-to-pick-up list, and every line of the tree including each parent's `_(done/total)_` tuple.

**What is checked but not owned** — the "Answers" column is compared on its count cell only. Prose
outside the derived blocks is untouched.

## The header now tells the truth

ST-10 required that the page stop claiming to be generated by something that does not exist. It now
reads as hand-written and machine-checked, and names the checker. `test/backlog-tracker.test.mjs`
asserts both halves: that the tracker names `scripts/backlog.mjs`, and that the words `GENERATED
FILE` are gone.

## Run 1 — the checker against the tracker as committed

Before any item was changed, against `main`'s tracker:

```text
$ node scripts/backlog.mjs
artifacts/backlog/README.md agrees with items/ — 32 items, 12/23 leaves complete.
exit 0
```

Worth recording for what it is: the three hand-recomputations that preceded this were *correct*. The
defect was never that a number was wrong. It was that nothing could have told us if one had been.

## Run 2 — closing ST-10 breaks nine figures, and the checker names all nine

ST-10's own closure is the first change the checker ever had to police. Setting its status to
`COMPLETE` moved nine derived figures:

```text
$ node scripts/backlog.mjs
artifacts/backlog/README.md disagrees with items/ in 9 place(s):

  leaf-completion             13 of 23 — 57%          (README said 12 of 23 — 52%)
  progress-bar                57% bar                 (README said 52%)
  status-count:NOT_STARTED    7                       (README said 8)
  status-count:COMPLETE       15                      (README said 14)
  theme-progress:TH-01        57% | 13 | 10           (README said 52% | 12 | 11)
  tree:TH-01                  _(13/23)_               (README said _(12/23)_)
  tree:IN-01                  _(13/23)_               (README said _(12/23)_)
  tree:EP-06                  _(1/3)_                 (README said _(0/3)_)
  tree:ST-10                  ●                       (README said ○)
exit 1
```

ST-10's own text predicted five hand-edits for adding two items. Closing one item moved nine
figures. The estimate in the finding was low, which is the ordinary way this kind of manual
procedure is underestimated.

## Mutation results — the checker is not decorative

"The current numbers match" is not evidence that a wrong number would be caught; a reconciliation
script that has stopped computing anything passes that test forever. So each derived figure is
broken at the source and the checker must name it. All mutations are applied to in-memory copies of
the item records; nothing is written to disk, and the tracker compared against is the real committed
one.

| Mutation | Must be reported as |
|---|---|
| A new story added under EP-06 | `status-total`, `type-count:story`, `tree-membership` |
| Each status, moved out of and into every bucket | `status-count:<from>` **and** `status-count:<to>` |
| A new item at each of five levels; the theme retyped | `type-count:<level>` for all six |
| A second child under EP-04 | `leaf-completion`, `progress-bar`, `theme-progress:TH-01` |
| ST-08 completed **and** FE-12 un-completed | `tree:EP-06`, `tree:EP-05`, and nothing else |
| EP-04 stopped **and** EP-05 started | `in-flight-membership`, and no status count |
| EP-01 reopened | `tree:EP-01`, `in-flight-membership`, two status counts — **not** leaf completion |
| An item retitled | `tree:ST-09` |
| An item marked `ready: true` | `ready-membership` |
| Eight figures corrupted in the tracker instead of the items | at least one finding each |
| Dangling parent · cycle · duplicate id · completion with no date · date with no completion · unknown status · missing title | a structural problem naming it |

Two of those are the point of the exercise.

**The aggregate-invariant pair.** Completing one leaf under EP-06 while un-completing one under
EP-05 leaves the status counts, the leaf ratio, the progress bar, the theme row and the in-flight set
bit-identical. Only two parent tuples move. The test asserts positively that `tree:EP-06` and
`tree:EP-05` fire *and* negatively that none of the five aggregates does. A checker that compares
totals sees nothing here at all.

**The EP-01 transition.** Reopening EP-01 must change its glyph, two status counts and the in-flight
set, and must leave leaf completion at 13/23 — because EP-01 is not a leaf. That pair of properties
is exactly what made EP-01's closure-by-owner-disposition legible in the first place. A checker that
cannot tell them apart cannot police the next such closure.

## Falsifiers — the mutations are load-bearing

Two independent amputations of the production module, each run against the unmodified test file.

**Falsifier A — the list and tree comparison neutered**, leaving only the tables and headline. This
is the shape a "do the totals match" checker would have:

```text
✔ backlog · the tracker's figures are the ones the item files imply     ← still green
✖ mutation · a changed item count is caught
✖ mutation · a parent progress tuple is caught even when every aggregate is unchanged
✖ mutation · the in-flight set is caught even when every status count is unchanged
✖ mutation · reopening EP-01 moves the parent reporting and leaves leaf completion alone
✖ mutation · a title, a glyph and a link are caught, not just the numbers
✖ mutation · marking an item ready is caught
✖ mutation · a hand-edited figure in the tracker is caught
7 failed
```

The first line is the finding. The primary check — the one that runs on every commit — **stays
green** under a checker that has stopped inspecting two thirds of the page. Only the mutations
distinguish the two. Without them, ST-10 would have been closed by something materially weaker than
what it asked for, and nothing would have said so.

**Falsifier B — the table and headline comparison neutered**, leaving only the lists and tree:

```text
✖ mutation · a changed item count is caught
✖ mutation · each status count is caught
✖ mutation · each hierarchy type count is caught
✖ mutation · the leaf count and the completion percentage are caught
✖ mutation · reopening EP-01 moves the parent reporting and leaves leaf completion alone
✖ mutation · a hand-edited figure in the tracker is caught
6 failed
```

Complementary sets, as they should be: neither half of the checker is redundant.

The tracker-corruption cases derive their own corruptions from the computed figures rather than
hardcoding literals, and each asserts the substitution actually applied. A corruption that silently
stops corrupting anything the day a number changes would be the same defect a third time.

## How it is wired into CI

`test/backlog-tracker.test.mjs` is an ordinary `node:test` file, so it runs under `npm test` — which
is what `ci/checks.sh` executes verbatim, and `ci/checks.sh` is the single check list that both
`scripts/ci.*` (container) and `.github/workflows/ci.yml` (hosted) run. No stage was added and no
command was restated: a backlog-changing commit cannot pass authoritative CI with a stale tracker,
on either runner, because the suite is red.

Deliberately **not** a shell stage. This repository's standing rule is that the verdict is driven by
a named test and never by a shell inspecting counts — the same reasoning `ci/checks.sh` gives for
treating its own pass/fail/skip numbers as report-only.

## No release entry

`VERSION` stays `0.5.0` and `CHANGELOG.md` is untouched, following the precedent set by the local-CI
merge `bf186e2`, which added `ci/`, `scripts/ci.*` and `scripts/submit-pr.*` without either. The
public contract is the state vocabulary, the exit codes, the result envelope and the CLI's accepted
arguments; a backlog checker is none of those. `scripts/backlog.mjs` is not exposed through `bin`.

## Boundaries

- **`scripts/backlog.mjs` is subject to ADR 0001's guard**, since `test/authority-boundary.test.mjs`
  scans every `.mjs` under `scripts/`. It passes: the checker names no pack, no native status and no
  pack evidence field. Placing it there rather than in `test-support/` was chosen partly for that —
  the constraint is appropriate and should apply.
- **`ready: true` is a new frontmatter key**, defined so that "Nothing marked ready" is a checkable
  assertion rather than an unfalsifiable one. Nothing sets it; the populated arm is exercised only by
  mutation, which is stated in the test rather than left implicit.
- **The frontmatter reader is strict and dependency-free.** A line it cannot parse is reported, never
  skipped — a parser that ignores what it does not understand turns a malformed item into an absent
  one, which is a wrong count that nothing reports.
- **A cycle is caught as unreachability from the root**, not by a dedicated cycle detector. Anything
  not reached is absent from every figure, so the two failures collapse into one report on purpose.
- **This does not make the backlog a system.** There is no schema file, no hierarchy validator beyond
  what is listed above, and no generator. ST-09 already records that a story parented directly to an
  initiative skips a level and that nothing validates it; that remains true and remains recorded.
