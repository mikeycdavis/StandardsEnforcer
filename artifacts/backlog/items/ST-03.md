## What

Released as `0.4.0`, breaking. Five pack-native states leave the public `STATE` surface in favour of
one state, `EVALUATED`, with passing riding beside it. Exit codes 2 and 3 are removed, leaving
`0` passing, `1` not passing, `4` not enforceable.

## Why

`COMPLIANT`, `NON_COMPLIANT`, `NOT_EVALUATED` and `BLOCKED_BY_INVARIANT` are MachineLearningStandards'
vocabulary, which six other packs do not share. Mapping `NOT_EVALUATED` to exit 2 kept the meaning in
encoded form — relocating the strings would have been cosmetic.

## Notes

**What is lost, said plainly:** a caller reading only an exit status can no longer distinguish "the
standards said no" from "the standards reached no conclusion". That distinction was never this
enforcer's to draw and it survives verbatim under `authority.status`. Only the lossy process
projection changed.

`describe()` also stopped printing a pack's score and passed/failed counts. MathematicsStandards
reports `score: 97` with 52 rules passed beside a status of `NOT_EVALUATED` — any line quoting that
number would have been quoting the most convincing false green available.

The evaluator seam became contract-driven in the same release: no hardcoded invocation, no probing,
no fallback, and no `adapterPath` parameter — because a mechanism for substituting another location
is a mechanism that gets used.
