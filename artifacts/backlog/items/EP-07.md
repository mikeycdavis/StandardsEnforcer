## What

The build and verification machinery itself: the entry points that stage a commit and start the
authoritative pipeline, the composition of the suite that pipeline runs, and this repository's own
tooling for the records it derives. This epic owns whether that machinery **works on the platforms
it claims, runs what it says it runs, and exists at all where a document claims to be produced by
it**.

## Why

[EP-06](EP-06.md) owns whether a green result is true. That question only arises for a run that
happened. [ST-09](ST-09.md), recorded 2026-08-16, is one step earlier than EP-06's property and was
filed with no epic that fits it: `scripts/ci.sh` cannot build the CI image under Git Bash on
Windows. It fails loudly at build time and certifies nothing, which is precisely why EP-06 is the
wrong owner: *"It does not certify something it failed to establish"* (ST-09's own rejection table).
That is about which script can start the pipeline, not about a claim being false.

**One independently earned specimen, not two.** [ST-13](ST-13.md) and [FE-23](FE-23.md) also sit
here, and neither was earned by the discriminator this epic states below. ST-13 — `npm test` was
`node --test` with no operand, so a hosted run recursively discovered `.oracle/test/**` and executed
MachineLearningStandards' suite alongside this one — argues itself onto EP-06's side of that line:
*"an assurance surface reporting more than it establishes"*. FE-23 is the same shape, a tracker
reporting figures as though computed. Both are here **by owner disposition**, and that disposition,
not the discriminator, is the authority for their membership. See *Scope boundary* below.

## Why not an existing epic

Created 2026-09-18 by owner disposition, after checking all six existing epics against ST-09 rather
than assuming none fit. ST-09 had already published four of these rows and declined to invent an
epic for one specimen; the fifth and sixth rows are added here.

| Epic | Its property | Why not |
| --- | --- | --- |
| EP-01 | authority transport under a pinned identity | Wrong axis entirely. Nothing here touches a pack, a release identity or an evaluator |
| EP-02 | whether a governed pull request can satisfy its own enforcement root | Unrelated — ST-09's row, unchanged |
| EP-03 | every repository has an explicit scope disposition | Unrelated, and COMPLETE since 2026-08-09 |
| EP-04 | this repository's own release identity — `VERSION`, `package.json`, `SCHEMA_VERSION` | Strictly versioning, not tooling — ST-09's row, unchanged |
| EP-05 | reach beyond one repository, one platform, one standard | *"'Platform' there means a second forge, not a second shell or OS"* — ST-09's row, unchanged. EP-05's `What` is the README's own gap list, which names none of this |
| EP-06 | this repository's assurance claims must establish what they report | The closest fit and still wrong for ST-09, per its own scope note forbidding a catch-all merely because a test is involved |

**The threshold ST-09 set has not been met on its own terms, and the claim that it had is
withdrawn.** ST-09 declined an epic on the grounds that *"One loud shell bug is not evidence for a
local-tooling epic; the next independently earned defect of the same kind would be."* This epic was
first written — and its creating change worded — as though [ST-13](ST-13.md) were that second
defect. It is not. ST-13 is a suite that reported more than it established, which is EP-06's side of
the discriminator below, and the owner placed it here as a reading of its **outcome** rather than as
a second specimen of ST-09's kind.

The honest account is therefore **one independently earned specimen — ST-09 — plus members placed
here by owner disposition**. What keeps this epic narrower than a catch-all is its stated scope and
the disposition recorded for each member, not a count of specimens.

## Scope boundary

This epic owns **the pipeline's ability to run, its composition, and this repository's own tooling
for the records it derives**. It does not own whether a run that completed reported truthfully —
that stays with EP-06, and a finding must not arrive here merely because CI was involved. The test
for which side a finding falls on is the one ST-09 used: did the machinery fail loudly, or did it
certify something it had not established?

**The owner approved this scope on 2026-09-20: it covers the backlog and tracker tooling, with
[ST-10](ST-10.md) under [FE-23](FE-23.md) placed here on that basis, and [ST-13](ST-13.md) under
[FE-25](FE-25.md) staying in the CI and build-reliability placement.** The approval widens what this
epic may hold; it does not turn either placement into a finding of the discriminator below.

**The third clause was added 2026-09-18 to admit [FE-23](FE-23.md), in the same change that needed
it.** That is recorded plainly, because a boundary widened to fit its first occupant is worth less
than one written in advance. The wording is taken from this epic's own `What` — the machinery this
repository is built and verified by — which `scripts/backlog.mjs` and `test/backlog-tracker.test.mjs`
are; the owner's instruction that the backlog tooling belongs under the epic responsible for
repository workflow tooling is what placed it here.

**Two of the three strands here are placed by disposition, and the discriminator above does not
reach them.** [ST-13](ST-13.md), under [FE-25](FE-25.md), argues it is EP-06's problem — *"an
assurance surface reporting more than it establishes, which is this epic's property exactly"* — and
that argument is not withdrawn; the owner's reading is that its outcome is the composition of the
suite. [FE-23](FE-23.md) is the same shape: a document that claimed to be generated when nothing
generated it is a false claim, and what sits here is its remedy, a repository script and the test
that runs it. For both, the owner's placement is the authority rather than the test above, and both
readings stay on the record.
