## What

The enforcer reads a pinned release's own `standards-adapter.json`, validates it against the schema,
confirms the entrypoint resolves inside the verified checkout, runs the declared invocation once, and
decides passing solely by membership of the set that release declared.

## Why

Any other arrangement puts a pack's semantics inside the enforcer. Hardcoding
`scripts/standards.mjs evaluate --dir=<target> --json` made the enforcer an authority on how a pack
is invoked; mapping `NOT_EVALUATED` to exit 2 made it an authority on what the answer meant.

## Notes

Three argv shapes — one positional, two flag-embedded — run through **one code path with no branch on
`standard.id` anywhere**, and `test/authority-boundary.test.mjs` enforces that structurally by
scanning `scripts/` for pack identities, native statuses, and pack evidence field names.

The strongest evidence is the open-vocabulary case: two packs declaring the *same two words* with
opposite passing sets, in the same run, producing opposite gate decisions. Nothing infers meaning
from spelling, even for a token decided the other way moments earlier.

The five blocked upstream authorities and ADR 0005 case 7b are recorded in the acceptance evidence
and are deliberately **not** tracked here — see [[EP-01]] for why.
