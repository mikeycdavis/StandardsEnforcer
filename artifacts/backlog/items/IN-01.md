## What

The three questions that have to be answered independently of the repository being governed —
*which released authority applies*, *what that authority actually said*, and *whether anything
the pull request could not touch required it to be asked at all* — plus the enforcer's own
release honesty and the reach it does not yet have.

## Why

Each of the three is separately forgeable, and forging any one of them is enough. A pack
invoked by a reimplementation, a verdict reinterpreted by the transport, and a required check
the pull request can satisfy for itself all end in the same place.

## Notes

The recurring finding across every epic here is the same one: checking a semantic against
reality rather than against its documentation has, so far, always found reality weaker. That
is the argument for the live experiments, and the reason an unexercised assurance is recorded
as unexercised rather than assumed.

Scope authority is deliberately not enforcement authority. `authoritative` means the
enforcement root verified and nothing else — a strong root makes the check unavoidable, it does
not make a partial detector more certain.
