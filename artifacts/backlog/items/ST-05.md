## What

The contract that decides how a pack is invoked and what counts as passing must come from the
verified release itself, and from nowhere the governed repository can reach.

## Why

[ADR 0005](../../adr/0005-adapter-provenance.md) — the governed repository supplies evidence, not
authority. A target that can supply the contract can invert `passing` and name its own evaluator.

## Notes

Nine hostile cases, executable: a forged adapter in the target, a forged adapter naming a fake
evaluator, an adapter in the target's parent directory, a pinned tag lacking a contract while `main`
has one, a contract at another tag of the same repository, a SHA disagreeing with the tag, entrypoint
escape, a working tree edited after materialisation, and a warm cache from another identity.

Case 6 is asserted by absence as well as by state — a failed identity produces a result carrying no
`report` and no `authority`, which is what makes "before any adapter load" checkable rather than
merely intended.

**Case 7b, symlinked entrypoint, is SKIPPED and not passed** — `EPERM` on this platform. Capability
is probed against a throwaway file separately from the fixture, so only *platform cannot create
symlinks* earns a skip; a capable platform where the fixture fails goes red. Not tracked as an item:
it needs a machine, not a decision.
