## What

Given an immutable *(standards repository, release tag, commit SHA)* identity, obtain that release's
own declared invocation contract, run exactly the evaluator it names, and carry its result back
without reinterpreting what the result means.

## Why

This is the load-bearing half of ADR 0001. If the enforcer knows what a pack's verdict means, it is
an authority on that pack's domain whether or not it contains any rules.

## Notes

Closed as **`IMPLEMENTED — PORTFOLIO_BLOCKED`**. Those are two claims and the milestone deliberately
records both: the architecture is accepted and demonstrated across three heterogeneous authorities
(Betting `v1.0.1`, Mathematics `v1.0.1`, MachineLearning `v1.5.0`), while universal portfolio
acceptance stays blocked on five upstream authorities.

**Two things are deliberately not backlog items**, per the acceptance record:

- The five blocked authorities (Innovation, Engineering, Health, Financial, Prediction) each fail for
  their own named reason and have owners who are not this repository. The reopening condition is
  mechanical — the next unmet prerequisite becomes the reported reason on the next run — so it does
  not depend on anyone remembering a ticket.
- ADR 0005 case 7b, symlink escape, is SKIPPED rather than passed: this platform returns `EPERM` on
  symlink creation. It needs a capable machine, not a decision. A skip on a capable environment is
  specified as a test failure, so the gap cannot decay into folklore.

The suite stands at 163 tests, 162 pass, 1 skipped. That qualification travels with the claim and
must not be compressed into "all provenance tests pass".

## Closed again 2026-08-12

**Closed deliberately, because the specific reason this epic was reopened has been resolved — not
because its children were all complete.** They were all complete on 2026-08-11 too, and that was
precisely when this epic had to be open.

The whole arc, kept rather than tidied away:

```text
2026-08-10  closed    IMPLEMENTED — PORTFOLIO_BLOCKED, on the evidence available then
2026-08-11  reopened  FE-13 falsified the parent's broader guarantee: pinned identity was
                      asserted by a marker on every run after the first, not established
2026-08-12  closed    0.4.1 shipped the remedy; FE-13 COMPLETE; pinned identity is again
                      established per run
```

The reopen is left visible on purpose. An epic that closed, was falsified, and closed again is a more
useful record than one that appears never to have been wrong — and the middle row is the only part of
this history that had to be discovered rather than planned.

What re-establishes this epic's identity guarantee — and only that —: `materialise()` no longer
returns on the presence of a marker. Every consumption re-establishes that the checkout is the
requested commit **and** that its tree matches, a rejected entry is rebuilt through a verified
staging swap rather than trusted or silently discarded, and the marker was moved outside the
checkout so that certifying a tree is not itself a modification of it. Evidence:
[`2026-08-12-fe13-cache-identity.md`](../../evidence/2026-08-12-fe13-cache-identity.md), `31fb5ec`.

Two qualifications travel with this closure and must not be dropped when it is cited:

- **The suite count is environment-dependent and must be dated, never quoted bare.** Measured on this
  branch on 2026-08-12: **183 tests, 182 passing, 1 skipped** — and the earlier `173/172/1` in this
  item was a measurement from before `0.5.0`, superseded. The one skip is ADR 0005 case 7b, a
  symlinked entrypoint that returns `EPERM` here; it is skipped and not passed, and this must never be
  compressed into "all identity tests pass". The deeper problem is that **23 further tests ran only
  because MachineLearningStandards happens to be on this disk** — on a machine without it they skip
  and the suite still exits 0. FE-14 on `reconciliation/m3-integration` owns that defect; this branch
  has no mechanism against it.
- **FE-13's red reproduction was executed but never committed while red.** Its source is not
  recoverable and is deliberately not being reconstructed. That is a limitation in the *process*
  record, not in the engineering result — the four originally failing behaviours are covered by
  successor cases under unchanged names — and it is recorded in the evidence file rather than here.
- **Nothing here changes EP-01's portfolio position or the enforcement root.** The 2026-08-10 state
  was `IMPLEMENTED — PORTFOLIO_BLOCKED` and it remains so: five upstream authorities are still
  blocked for their own reasons. [ST-07](ST-07.md) is untouched — there is still no live-validated
  authoritative GitHub Actions enforcement root, and this closure is not evidence about it in either
  direction. Closing this epic is not a step toward production readiness; it removes one known
  false-green path and nothing else.

Reopening conditions, so this closure is falsifiable rather than final: the acceptance property stops
being established; a successor test stops covering one of the original failing cases; or new evidence
shows a cached tree can again substitute for identity establishment at the moment of consumption.

## Reopened again 2026-08-12 — the closure above lasted about an hour

**The third reopening condition fired, from a direction the closure did not anticipate.** The closure
above was written and committed at `2a2a4fe`; evidence that falsifies its scope already existed on
`reconciliation/m3-integration`, and this line had not looked.

That is the finding, and it is not incidental. The closure asserted a guarantee re-established while
another line of the same repository held two open children of this epic. Nothing was hidden; nothing
had to be. **The closure was made without measuring the other lines**, which is the same failure the
epic itself is about — a verification that was true where it was taken and asserted somewhere wider.

### What falsified it

FE-15, *Concurrent processes share a mutable cache root* — on `reconciliation/m3-integration`, not on
this branch. Its property:

> Two independent StandardsEnforcer processes using the same shared cache root for the same release
> identity must both complete deterministically, neither executing unverified bytes, and neither
> invalidating the other's verified use.

Re-reproduced against post-`31fb5ec` code and **narrowed, not gone**: intra-run contention is solved
by per-process staging; two concurrent suite *processes* still reproduce it, as a single
`STANDARDS_IDENTITY_MISMATCH` where `EVALUATED` was expected.

### The distinction that keeps FE-13 intact

```text
FE-13   a cache hit cannot substitute for identity establishment at consumption
        time, on the implemented single-process materialisation path
        → COMPLETE, and it stays COMPLETE

FE-15   concurrent processes can still share and mutate one cache root with no
        cross-process ownership guarantee
        → open

EP-01   authority transport under a pinned identity
        → broader than FE-13's single-process repair
        → must stay open while FE-15 is a child
```

**FE-13 and `0.4.1` are not invalidated, and nothing about them is retracted.** New evidence exposed a
second failure mode under the same parent guarantee. This is exactly the movement that reopened this
epic on 2026-08-11, occurring a second time — and the FE-13 evidence file already named this residual
in its own words: *"there is no cross-process lock… the window is narrowed rather than closed."* FE-15
is that residual, tracked, on a line this one had not read.

**Note the change in failure mode**, because it decides what the remedy may do. The pre-`31fb5ec`
defect was false assurance; the current one is a **false negative** — the second process refused to
execute bytes whose identity it could not establish, which is the security property working. What
fails now is determinism and operational isolation. A remedy must fix that *without* weakening the
identity boundary, and a lock that becomes a reason to skip verification would rebuild
`.enforcer-complete` under a new name.

### What is not being done

- **FE-15 is not being reparented to preserve this closure.** Its subject is whether a consumed
  checkout still proves the requested pinned identity, which is this epic's guarantee.
- **FE-14 is not being adopted here by inheritance.** Its parentage on the other line is not evidence;
  it will be justified from its own acceptance property or moved.
- **Neither item is being copied onto this branch**, and no branch is being reconciled. That is
  sequenced work under the re-scoped FE-06.

## Reconciliation note — 2026-08-12, source `670d617`

So the literal condition the reopen cited — *"must stay open while FE-15 is a child [and open]"* — no
longer holds. **That is exactly the condition under which this epic must not close itself.** All
enumerated children were complete on 2026-08-11 too, and on 2026-08-12 before FE-15 was discovered; on
both occasions the completion claim was wrong. A third closure decided by arithmetic over child
statuses would repeat the error the previous two taught, so the status is held for an owner decision
rather than derived.

What a closing decision would now need to weigh, stated so it can be made rather than deferred
indefinitely:

- **The concurrency property is established here for the first time.** The FE-15 falsifier was
  reproduced against `b61841f` before its remedy crossed, and its arms 2/2b — a tampered entry is not
  trusted however it came to be there — still pass **with** the publish lock in place, so coordination
  did not become a reason to skip verification.
- **The reproduction has a setup-sensitive detection surface.** Run 1 was green on a cold cache; runs
  2–6 were deterministically red. A single-run verification would have concluded the defect did not
  reproduce. Any future claim about this property must state how many runs it rests on.
- **What remains unestablished is not owned here.** ST-07's enforcement root and FE-14's CI evidence
  gap are EP-02's and EP-06's respectively. Neither blocks this epic, and neither is closed by it.

## Closed 2026-08-16 — owner disposition

**Not closed because all children are complete.** They were complete at both previous closures, and
both were wrong. This closure answers the question the reconciliation note above left for an owner:
whether the authority-transport guarantee itself is established.

It is, at `3ae72c9`, against every falsifier currently known:

```text
FE-13  a cache marker could substitute for current identity verification
       → repaired; every consumption re-establishes HEAD and tree identity

FE-15  concurrent processes could invalidate one another's verified use
       → repaired without weakening verification; a tampered entry is still
         re-verified with the publish lock in place

PR #1  a symlinked evaluator entrypoint could escape the pinned release and
       execute foreign bytes
       → reproduced RED against the pre-fix implementation, repaired under the
         existing containment invariant, and exercised in authoritative
         container CI with zero skips
```

**The third falsifier is why this closure rests on more than the first two did.** EP-01's `What` is
not only about cache identity: it requires that the enforcer *run exactly the evaluator the pinned
release names*. A symlinked entrypoint resolving outside the verified checkout falsified that
directly — the foreign evaluator ran and its report was accepted as the authority's verdict. The
containment defect arrived after the reconciliation note was written, from work that was not looking
for it, and it is included in this decision rather than left to a later one.

### What this closure does not claim

**Not that authority transport cannot be falsified again.** This epic's own history demonstrates the
opposite twice over, and a closure asserting finality would be the least defensible thing in the
record. What is claimed is narrower:

> At `3ae72c9`, every known falsifier of EP-01's acceptance property has been resolved, and no known
> open item currently falsifies that property.

**Reopening stays evidence-triggered, and remains normal rather than embarrassing.** Reopen this epic
if a path is found where bytes outside the verified release can execute, where cached materialisation
can substitute for identity establishment, or where the enforcer reinterprets an authority's native
semantics. Three reopenings would be the record working.

### Gaps that are real and are not this epic's

```text
ST-08   whether CI keeps exercising the symlink control        → EP-06
ST-07   the external enforcement root                          → EP-02
five upstream authorities without adapters                     → PORTFOLIO_BLOCKED
```

**ST-08 is the closest call and is correctly outside.** It asks whether StandardsEnforcer's own CI
will continue to exercise the symlink control — a self-assurance property. The product defect it
would guard against is repaired here; an unasserted capability is EP-06's subject, not this one's.
The five unsupported authorities remain the `PORTFOLIO_BLOCKED` half of a distinction this epic
established on 2026-08-10, not evidence that the architecture is incomplete.
